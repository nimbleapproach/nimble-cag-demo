The Eternal City's Table: The Robust Simplicity of Roman Cuisine

Roman cuisine, the gastronomic heart of Lazio, is a testament to the Eternal City's enduring spirit: robust, unpretentious, and deeply satisfying. It reflects a history of resourceful simplicity, transforming humble ingredients into dishes of profound flavour. At Ristorante del Cuore, we honour this tradition by crafting classic Roman dishes that evoke the vibrant energy of a Roman trattoria.

Key Principles of Roman Cuisine:

La Cucina Povera with Punch: Like other regional Italian cuisines, Roman cooking emerged from the concept of cucina povera, but with a distinctive bold flavour profile. Dishes often feature simple, local ingredients elevated by strong cheeses, cured meats, and pungent herbs.

Pasta as King: Rome is famed for its iconic pasta dishes, often united by a few key ingredients: Pecorino Romano cheese, Guanciale (cured pork jowl), and black pepper.

Art of Offal: Reflecting its humble origins and a nose-to-tail philosophy, Roman cuisine makes masterful use of quinto quarto (the "fifth quarter" – offal). Dishes like Coda alla Vaccinara (oxtail stew) and Trippa alla Romana (tripe) are deeply flavourful and integral to the tradition.

Fresh Vegetables: Artichokes (Carciofi alla Romana or alla Giudìa), chicory, and broad beans are staple vegetables, often prepared simply to highlight their natural taste.

Olive Oil and Herbs: High-quality olive oil and fresh herbs like mint, parsley, and mentuccia (Roman mint) are essential flavour enhancers.

Signature Roman Dishes on Our Menu:

We proudly present the pillars of Roman pasta, prepared with authentic ingredients:

Cacio e Pepe: A deceptively simple yet utterly profound dish of pasta (typically tonnarelli) tossed with freshly ground black pepper, Pecorino Romano cheese, and a touch of pasta cooking water, creating a creamy, emulsified sauce.

Carbonara: Our authentic version uses guanciale, eggs (no cream!), Pecorino Romano, and black pepper, creating a rich, silky sauce.

Amatriciana: A robust sauce featuring guanciale, Pecorino Romano, black pepper, and ripe tomatoes, typically served with bucatini or rigatoni.

Gricia: Often called "white Amatriciana," it's the progenitor of Amatriciana and Carbonara, with guanciale, Pecorino Romano, and black pepper, but no tomato or egg.

Saltimbocca alla Romana: Veal cutlets topped with prosciutto and sage, pan-fried in white wine and butter, a quick and flavourful main course.
